Sigiriya - The Masterpiece Artwork in Sri Lankan History

Sigiriya is one of the most valuable historical monuments of Sri Lanka. Sigiriya is probably the most visited tourist destination for more than a thousand years. It's located in Matale District (Central Province) of Sri Lanka. Sigiriya is now listed as a World Heritage site by UNESCO since 1982. The rock fortress was built by King Kashyapa (477 - 495 AD). It had been designed in the form of a huge stone lion, however only the feet has survived and the upper part of the body has been destroyed. After King Kashyapa's death Sigiriya became a Buddhist monastery. Sigiriya is mainly famous for,

  • Sigiri Frescoes,
  • Sigiri Mirror Wall,
  • Remnants of a ruined Palace on top of the rock,
  • Networks of gardens including fortifications, ponds, canals and fountains.

Sigiriya Frescoes

The Sigiriya Frescoes found on the Western side of the rock are considered to be drawn in the 5th Century. Some say that the figures represent the celestial nymphs(Sigiri Apsaras) carrying flowers. While others suggest that they are queens of King Kashyapa. During King Kashyapa's reign there were approximately 500 paintings. But at the time of discovery, most of the paintings were destroyed, and only 18 paintings have survived upto this date. These paintings have attracted people from all over the country and the world .

Sigiriya Mirror Wall

In good old days it was polished so thoroughly that the King could see his reflection through it. The Mirror Wall is painted with inscriptions and poems written by the visitors of Sigiriya. As per architects the most ancient inscriptions are dated from the 8th century. They mainly expressed their feelings, love, irony by writing poems on the Mirror Wall. The government has taken actions to prevent further writings on the Mirror Wall for the protection of the old writings.

Remnants of the ruined Palace

Although the fortress that has remnants of a ruined palace it's found on the summit of the Sigiriya Rock. The foundations of earlier buildings can still be seen. When you once climb up the Sigiriya rock you get the chance to get a panaromic view of the surroundings of the rock fortress. The strong winds will give an unforgettable experience.

Surrounding Landscape

Since it's build by King Kashyapa as his kingdom it has a gignatic view from 200 feet above the surrounding area. It had been consisted of ponds, cave and boulder gardens and also terraced gardens. The kingdom was surrounded by ramparts, canals, locks, lakes, dams, bridges which added beauty as well as security to the palace and the kingdom. This has become a tourist attraction because of the architectural capabilities our Kings possessed in urban planning, water engineering, horticulture and arts.
